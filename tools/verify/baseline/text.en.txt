About
I am a Ph.D. student (D1) at Toyota Technological Institute, advised by Prof. Norimichi Ukita in the Intelligent Information Media Lab. My research builds human- and scene-interactive, physics-aware systems for predicting how people move.
I am a JSPS Research Fellow (DC1) and have published as first author at top-tier venues including CVPR and WACV. I completed a research internship at Woven by Toyota, and I serve the community as Tech Chair for ACCV 2026.

↑ Behind this page, a crowd is crossing. Every walker anticipates when a collision would happen rather than reacting to how close someone is, following the power law Karamouzas, Skinner and Guy fitted to real pedestrians (PRL 113, 2014) — so paths that merely pass close by are ignored, and a real collision course is answered early and to one side. Nothing is drawn by hand and no route is scripted: the lanes, the wake behind each pillar, the way a pair walking abreast breaks into single file when it gets tight, all fall out of the forces. The orange walker also forecasts its own next few seconds, rolled out through the same model against the crowd actually around it.
